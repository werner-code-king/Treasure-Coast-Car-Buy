Data & Methodology

The Value Score (0–100) blends five factors, each normalized against every trim/vehicle in this comparison so the best performer in a category scores highest. The formula and weights are unchanged from the original compact-SUV comparison; midsize 3-row SUVs are scored the same way, so a larger, pricier SUV will naturally score lower on price and cargo-behind-2nd-row even if it's the right vehicle for a 3-row need — use the Class filter to compare like-for-like.

Reliability, resale, maintenance, insurance, and cargo are researched at the model/powertrain level, not per individual trim (these generally don't change trim to trim) — only price does. That means within one model, a higher trim will always score lower on Value than its cheaper sibling, since the formula only sees a higher price for the same core vehicle; it can't credit the extra features, materials, or capability that trim adds. Use the Value Score to compare across different models, and the spec sheet itself to judge whether a pricier trim's added content is worth it.

  • Reliability — 25%: J.D. Power / Consumer Reports / Edmunds ratings, converted to a 0–100 scale.
  • 5-year resale value — 20%: percentage of MSRP retained after 5 years.
  • Maintenance cost — 20%: estimated annual maintenance/repair cost (lower is better).
  • Cargo space — 15%: cubic feet behind the 2nd row.
  • Starting price — 20%: lower MSRP scores higher (affordability).

Fields marked est. are analyst estimates derived from qualitative source language (e.g. "strong resale value") because no single directly comparable figure was published across every vehicle. All other figures are stated verbatim by the cited source. Prices, incentives, and dealer inventory change frequently — always confirm current pricing and availability with the dealership before purchase.
